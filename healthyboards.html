Healthy Boards

Clarity, competency, and chemistry: governance worth gathering for.

Our Point of View

Clarity. Competency. Chemistry.

Healthy boards do not happen because the right people sit at the table. They happen because three things are tended to with rigor, and most boards underperform on at least one without a structured way to find out which.

CLARITY

What we are deciding, and how.

COMPETENCY

Who is at the table, per the matrix.

CHEMISTRY

The trust to say the hard thing.

Sound Familiar?

Six things we hear before the work begins.

Healthy Boards is built for boards that are meant to be governing and are mostly attending, in founder-led companies, mission-driven organizations, family offices, and institutions alike.

Our bylaws say two terms. Some members have been here seven years.

The mechanism to refresh the board has rusted shut.

One of our directors is also a vendor. Everyone knows. No one says it.

The conflict policy is in the bylaws; the recusals are not actually happening.

Our board debates operating detail and calls it strategy.

No one is sure where governance ends and management begins.

The chair and I are cordial. We are not partners.

The CEO-Chair relationship has become a calendar event, not a thought partnership.

Every open seat goes to someone the chair knows.

Rolodex recruiting: no competency matrix, no gap analysis, no bench design.

Everyone is pleasant. No one says the hard thing.

Consensus gets manufactured in the room; governance happens somewhere else, or not at all.

What Changes

What is different at close.

Every lever moves the board from attending to actually governing.

Clarity

Clean Decision Rights

Today“What are we actually deciding?” Agendas that drift, decision rights nobody can name.

At closeClean decision rights and crisp agendas: the board knows what is theirs, and what is not.

Chemistry

The Room Where the Hard Thing Gets Said

TodayPolite, not trusting: consensus manufactured in the room, candor saved for the parking lot.

At closeThe room where the hard thing gets said: trust that makes candor normal.

If your board attends but does not yet govern, that is the conversation we would welcome.

One call. No pitch. A candid read on whether the fit is real.
